01 · The mandate

Start from the
executive mandate.

Olist operates as a B2B2C marketplace aggregator: it enables thousands of small Brazilian merchants to sell through unified storefronts and ship via third-party carriers. Raw transaction logs record 99,441 orders across nine tables, but leadership cannot steer a P&L from raw logs when order grains, customer identities and logistics timestamps are fragmented.

So before writing a single analytical query, I organised the work around four questions that determine marketplace survival: the four lenses a CEO, CFO, COO and CCO would each put on this business.

Four questions that decide survival

Growth architectureIs top-line GMV compounding organically, or masking an acquisition-driven plateau?
Fulfilment & unit economicsWhere do broken delivery promises and freight ratios destroy margin?
Retention & LTVWhy do 96.9% of buyers never return, and what operational threshold triggers churn?
Supply risk & basket sizeHow exposed is GMV to top-seller churn, and why are baskets stuck at one item?

The order matters: growth and fulfilment are P&L questions the board can read this quarter; retention and supply are risk questions that decide whether the business survives the next two years.

What the work had to deliver

  • MandateDiagnose why a 7.5× growth marketplace stalled in 2018, and design a plan for its 96.9% buyer churn, its 24% peak state-level late rate and its 67.6% seller concentration.
  • Evidence99,441 orders across 96,096 unique buyers and 3,095 sellers (September 2016 to October 2018). All monetary figures in Brazilian reais (R$).
  • DeliverableA prioritised commercial roadmap, with every recommendation tied to a KPI the board can watch and a stop rule. Built on SQL Server and Python, but designed for the decision, not the query.

Audit the raw transaction ledger

A silent data error today becomes a wrong decision tomorrow, and a stakeholder only discovers it after the budget is committed. So before any insight is drawn, the ledger has to be trusted. I ingested all nine operational exports into olist_db with strict financial and temporal typing (DECIMAL(10,2) for currency, DATETIME2 for logistics milestones), then ran an automated partition audit over sys.partitions to confirm no records were silently dropped.

The audit surfaces the first strategic trap: geolocation is 64% of all rows because each zip code appears many times. Joining orders straight to raw locations would multiply GMV and order counts across every regional report, quietly inflating a state's performance and misdirecting carrier investment.

    s.name AS Schema_Name,
    p.rows AS Row_Count,
    CASE
        WHEN p.rows = 0 THEN 'EMPTY - CHECK IMPORT'
        ELSE 'OK'
    END AS Status
FROM sys.tables t
INNER JOIN sys.schemas s ON t.schema_id = s.schema_id
INNER JOIN sys.partitions p ON t.object_id = p.object_id
WHERE s.name = 'olist'
  AND p.index_id IN (0, 1)
ORDER BY p.rows DESC;

Eliminate reporting distortions

Noise upstream is strategy risk downstream: a double-counted state inflates a regional SLA story, an unmapped category hides a P&L problem, and duplicate reviews move NPS enough to justify or kill a retention programme. Before modelling the warehouse, I resolved three distortions.

Regional GMV fan-out

Raw distortion

1,000,163 location rows with multiple coordinates per zip code

Governance fix

Collapsed to 19,015 unique zip centroids with modal city and state attribution

Unattributed revenue

Raw distortion

73 Portuguese category names and 610 unmapped products

Governance fix

Standardised to 71 English categories, 2 retained Portuguese niches and an explicit "Unknown" bucket

Skewed NPS & CSAT

Raw distortion

99,224 review rows containing 814 duplicate review IDs

Governance fix

Deduplicated to 98,410 unique reviews, keeping only the latest customer timestamp

Corrupted carrier timestamps, where delivery dates appeared earlier than purchase dates, are also quarantined to NULL, so impossible negative lead times never flatter regional transit averages.

WITH Geo_Frequency AS (
    SELECT
        geolocation_zip_code_prefix,
        geolocation_city,
        geolocation_state,
        COUNT(*) as freq,
        ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY geolocation_zip_code_prefix ORDER BY COUNT(*) DESC) as rn
    FROM olist.geolocation
    GROUP BY geolocation_zip_code_prefix, geolocation_city, geolocation_state
),
Geo_Centroids AS (
    SELECT
        geolocation_zip_code_prefix,
        AVG(geolocation_lat) as avg_lat,
        AVG(geolocation_lng) as avg_lng
    FROM olist.geolocation
    GROUP BY geolocation_zip_code_prefix
)
SELECT
    c.geolocation_zip_code_prefix AS zip_code,
    c.avg_lat,
    c.avg_lng,
    f.geolocation_city AS city,
    f.geolocation_state AS state
INTO dwh.Dim_Geolocation
FROM Geo_Centroids c
JOIN Geo_Frequency f ON c.geolocation_zip_code_prefix = f.geolocation_zip_code_prefix
WHERE f.rn = 1;

03 · Warehouse & semantic layer

Model the grain,
then lock the KPIs.

Grain is a commercial choice

Mixing multi-item line rows with order-level delivery metrics is the fastest way to double-count revenue and distort late-delivery rates, the two numbers the whole logistics case rests on. So I separated Fact_Orders (99,441 rows, item prices and freight pre-aggregated to the exact order grain) from Fact_Order_Items (112,650 rows, item-level detail for seller share, freight ratios and cross-sell attach).

Identify the real buyer for LTV

Olist assigns a new customer_id to every checkout, so 99,441 transaction IDs belong to 96,096 unique individuals (customer_unique_id). Modelling Dim_Customer around the person rather than the checkout token is what exposes the platform's true repeat-purchase economics. Get this wrong and the churn story disappears.

Read the SQL: pre-aggregating financials to protect the Fact_Orders grain 
-- FIX: Calculate Financials first (Pre-Aggregation)
-- We must aggregate items to the Order grain to join them to Fact_Orders
WITH Order_Financials AS (
    SELECT
        order_id,
        SUM(price) AS total_order_value,
        SUM(freight_value) AS total_freight,
        COUNT(*) AS items_count
    FROM olist.order_items
    GROUP BY order_id
)
SELECT
    o.order_id,
    o.customer_id,
    o.order_status,
    o.order_purchase_timestamp,

    -- Financials (Added)
    ISNULL(f.total_order_value, 0) AS total_order_value,
    ISNULL(f.total_freight, 0) AS total_freight,
    ISNULL(f.items_count, 0) AS items_count,

    -- Logistics
    CASE
        WHEN o.order_delivered_customer_date < o.order_purchase_timestamp THEN NULL
        ELSE DATEDIFF(DAY, o.order_purchase_timestamp, o.order_delivered_customer_date)
    END AS actual_lead_time_days,

    DATEDIFF(DAY, o.order_delivered_customer_date, o.order_estimated_delivery_date) AS delivery_gap_days,

    -- Flags
    CASE WHEN o.order_status = 'delivered' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS is_delivered,
    CASE WHEN o.order_status IN ('canceled', 'unavailable') THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS is_canceled,

    CASE
        WHEN o.order_delivered_customer_date > o.order_estimated_delivery_date THEN 1
        ELSE 0
    END AS is_late_delivery,

    -- Dimensions
    c.customer_city,
    c.customer_state,
    c.customer_zip_code_prefix AS customer_zip

INTO dwh.Fact_Orders
FROM olist.orders o
LEFT JOIN olist.customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
LEFT JOIN Order_Financials f ON o.order_id = f.order_id;

Lock KPI definitions into one semantic view

Different teams, different numbers, is the fastest way to lose a board. When Finance, Operations and Marketing each write their own joins, meetings turn into debates over whose numbers are right. I built vw_order_metrics as a governed layer at one row per order (purchase_ts, gmv, delivery_gap_days) that enforces two company-wide rules in code.

Primary category attribution

Multi-item orders are attributed to the category of their highest-value item (ORDER BY price DESC). That prevents double-counted order volume across category managers and gives each category owner a clean, honest P&L.

Standardised NPS buckets

Review scores of 1–2 stars are Detractor, 4–5 stars Promoter and 3 stars Passive. That ties logistics SLA breaches directly to customer sentiment, so the retention programme has a live read-out.

Read the SQL: category attribution and NPS classification 
    -- Product Category Logic:
    -- An order might have multiple items. We pick the category of the most expensive item
    -- to define the "Primary Category" of the order.
    (SELECT TOP 1 p.category_name
     FROM dwh.Fact_Order_Items foi
     JOIN dwh.Dim_Products p ON foi.product_id = p.product_id
     WHERE foi.order_id = f.order_id
     ORDER BY foi.price DESC) AS category_name,

    -- …

    -- Quality Metrics
    r.review_score,
    CASE
        WHEN r.review_score <= 2 THEN 'Detractor'
        WHEN r.review_score >= 4 THEN 'Promoter'
        ELSE 'Passive'
    END AS nps_bucket

Each pillar runs the same discipline: identify the problem, find the pain point, show the solution, define the operations. Fourteen diagnostic queries across dwh, from parts B and C of 03_business_queries.sql.

01

Growth architecture: 7.5× expansion, then an acquisition plateau.

Q1Q3
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    On the surface, delivered GMV grew 7.5× from R$112K in January 2017 to R$839K in August 2018. The monthly trajectory reveals a structural stall: after spiking to R$991K during Black Friday (November 2017), revenue flattened between R$0.83M and R$0.98M a month throughout 2018, bringing January–August 2018 growth down to 2.4× year on year.

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    Olist is running on a feast-or-famine acquisition treadmill. Revenue is anchored by three hero verticals: Health & Beauty (R$1.23M), Watches & Gifts (R$1.17M) and Bed, Bath & Table (R$1.03M). Yet because 96.9% of buyers never place a second order, past cohorts add almost no compounding baseline to 2018 GMV. When paid acquisition or seasonal traffic slows, the top line flattens immediately.

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    Shift capital from broad seasonal acquisition spikes to category-led lifecycle compounding: use natural replenishment cycles in Health & Beauty (the #1 revenue vertical) as the primary repeat-purchase engine, and Bed, Bath & Table as the anchor for multi-item home bundles, turning one-off holiday traffic into recurring quarterly demand.

  4. 04

    Define the operations

    Re-anchor executive reporting in vw_order_metrics to split monthly GMV into first-order GMV and repeat-cohort GMV by primary category. Ring-fence CRM replenishment triggers at Day 30 and Day 60 for delivered Health & Beauty buyers, and require pre-committed holdout lift targets before approving Q4 promotional subsidies.

Read the SQL: Q3 monthly revenue trajectory 
-- Q3. Monthly Sales Trend (Seasonality)
SELECT
    FORMAT(purchase_date, 'yyyy-MM') AS Sales_Month,
    FORMAT(SUM(gmv), 'C', 'en-US') AS Revenue
FROM dwh.vw_order_metrics
WHERE order_status = 'delivered' AND purchase_date >= '2017-01-01'
GROUP BY FORMAT(purchase_date, 'yyyy-MM')
ORDER BY Sales_Month DESC;

02

Fulfilment & unit economics: carrier bottlenecks and the freight trap.

Q2Q7Q9Q10Q11
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    Across Brazil, 8.1% of delivered orders miss the promised delivery date, while 2,963 orders (3.0%) never reach "delivered" at all: direct GMV and trust leakage. At the same time, shipping costs relative to item prices are choking conversion in low-ticket categories.

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    Splitting order timestamps into three fulfilment stages proves that sellers are not the bottleneck: payment approval takes under a day and merchants hand parcels to carriers within 2.5–3.3 days. The failure sits in carrier line-haul transit (20–26 days in the slowest states), pushing late rates to 24.0% in Alagoas and 15.2–19.6% across Maranhão, Piauí, Ceará and Sergipe, while São Paulo (2,391 late orders) and Rio de Janeiro (1,671) generate the largest absolute volume. Worse, 1,101 orders have been stuck at "shipped" for 60+ days, and in Electronics (median item price R$21.90) freight averages 68.8% of the product price, costing more than the item itself on 22% of orders.

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    A three-part logistics and freight re-architecture: (1) replace uniform national checkout ETAs with dynamic regional carrier buffers in the Northeast and Rio de Janeiro; (2) move carrier contracts to performance-tiered SLAs rather than penalising merchants who already pack in under 3.3 days; (3) introduce a minimum cart threshold for flat-rate shipping on small Electronics, so buyers bundle accessories rather than abandoning R$21.90 items over freight shock.

  4. 04

    Define the operations

    Implement an automated 30-day in-transit exception trigger in SQL to flag lost shipments, start carrier claims and auto-refund customers, catching the 1,101 stranded orders before they become chargebacks. Track Seller Pack SLA (under 3 days) separately from Carrier Transit SLA by state route, and reject premature warehouse CAPEX in the North: Roraima, Amapá and Amazonas totalled just 254 orders over two years, far below break-even density.

Read the SQL: Q11 stage-by-stage bottleneck split 
-- Q11. Logistics Breakdown (Bottleneck Analysis)
-- Insight: Does the delay happen at Approval, Packing (Seller), or Transit (Carrier)?
SELECT TOP 5
    customer_state,
    AVG(DATEDIFF(HOUR, purchase_ts, approved_ts))/24.0 as Days_To_Approve,
    AVG(DATEDIFF(HOUR, approved_ts, carrier_ts))/24.0 as Days_To_Pack,
    AVG(DATEDIFF(HOUR, carrier_ts, delivered_ts))/24.0 as Days_In_Transit
FROM dwh.vw_order_metrics
WHERE order_status = 'delivered'
GROUP BY customer_state
ORDER BY Days_In_Transit DESC;

03

Retention & channel fit: the 96.9% churn wall and the 20-day cliff.

Q4Q5Q6Q14
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    Olist is functioning as a one-time customer rental channel, not a compounding marketplace: of 96,096 unique buyers, 93,099 (96.9%) purchased only once, leaving just 2,997 repeat customers across two years. When LTV is capped at a single order, acquisition cost cannot be amortised: the growth in finding 01 is being bought, not compounded.

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    Joining Fact_Orders to Dim_Reviews pinpoints where trust breaks: the 20-day transit cliff. Detractor rates (1–2 stars) stay contained at 7.2% for 0–5 day deliveries (4.44★), 8.4% for 6–10 days (4.35★) and 10.5% for 11–20 days (4.21★), then jump 3.5× to 37.0% (3.16★) beyond 20 days. Demand is skewed to weekdays: about 175 orders a day on Mondays and Tuesdays against 123 on Saturdays, with the busiest weekday block between 10:00 and 16:00. And 19.9% of orders rely on boleto bank slips (against 76.9% on credit cards), adding multi-day clearing delays before a parcel even ships.

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    Replace blanket win-back discounting with experience-gated CRM and workday channel alignment: (1) gate second-order incentives to buyers whose first order arrived within 10 days (4.35–4.44★ promoters), avoiding subsidy waste on detractors burned by 20+ day transit; (2) move paid marketing from weekend slots into Monday–Friday daytime windows (10:00–16:00) while validating how much of that demand is B2B or office procurement; (3) offer instant-checkout incentives to move boleto users onto faster-clearing payment rails.

  4. 04

    Define the operations

    Automate cohort segmentation inside vw_order_metrics using actual_lead_time_days <= 10 and nps_bucket = 'Promoter'. Run a controlled 90-day A/B holdout on post-delivery repeat vouchers, add a lightweight CNPJ (business tax ID) toggle at checkout to size B2B demand before building dedicated features, and cap boleto inventory holds at 48 hours.

Read the SQL: Q5 retention across unique individuals 
-- Q5. Customer Retention Rate (Lifetime Value Check)
WITH CustomerFrequency AS (
    SELECT customer_unique_id, COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) as Lifetime_Orders
    FROM dwh.Dim_Customer c
    JOIN dwh.Fact_Orders f ON c.customer_id = f.customer_id
    GROUP BY customer_unique_id
)
SELECT
    CASE WHEN Lifetime_Orders = 1 THEN 'One-Time Buyer' ELSE 'Repeat Buyer' END AS Buyer_Type,
    COUNT(*) as Customer_Count,
    FORMAT(COUNT(*) * 1.0 / (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM CustomerFrequency), 'P') as Share_of_Base
FROM CustomerFrequency
GROUP BY CASE WHEN Lifetime_Orders = 1 THEN 'One-Time Buyer' ELSE 'Repeat Buyer' END;

04

Supply concentration & baskets: protect the top 10%, unlock attach.

Q8Q12Q13
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    Olist faces severe supply-side concentration alongside under-monetised baskets: the top 10% of sellers (310 of 3,095) control 67.6% of item revenue, the top 100 alone generate 45.1%, and the bottom 50% contribute just 3.2%. At the same time, 96.7% of orders contain a single distinct product.

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    This Pareto skew creates two vulnerabilities. First, if competing Brazilian marketplaces poach even 20–30 of the top 100 sellers, platform GMV drops immediately: the concentration is the risk. Second, seller volume against review scores exposes brand-damaging volume: one top-20 seller averages 3.35★ across 982 orders, and five mid-to-large sellers (50+ orders) average under 3.0★ while contributing only 0.6% of revenue. On the demand side, discovery fails to cross-sell: only 3.3% of orders include two distinct products and 0.8% span two categories, led by Bed, Bath & Table with Furniture & Décor at 70 orders.

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    Tiered seller portfolio governance and cross-category merchandising: (1) lock in the top 100–310 anchor sellers with key-account management, faster payouts and volume-tiered commission rebates, while incubating decile 2–3 merchants (22.8% of revenue) to spread concentration risk; (2) put the five sub-3.0★ high-volume sellers on a 30-day quality remediation gate, risking just 0.6% of revenue to remove thousands of detractor experiences; (3) deploy "Frequently Bought Together" bundles pairing Bed, Bath & Table with Furniture & Décor and Housewares at checkout.

  4. 04

    Define the operations

    Run automated monthly NTILE(10) seller scorecards in the warehouse tracking revenue share, cancellation rate, pack SLA and rolling 90-day review average. Set a hard rule: any seller above 50 orders with a rating below 3.5★ is throttled in search impressions until SLA compliance is restored. Measure the cross-sell module by multi-SKU attach rate (from 3.3% toward 6%+) and incremental AOV against a checkout holdout group.

Read the SQL: Q8 seller deciles across 3,095 sellers 
-- Q8. Seller Concentration (Pareto Principle)
WITH SellerSales AS (
    SELECT seller_id, SUM(price) as Revenue
    FROM dwh.Fact_Order_Items
    GROUP BY seller_id
),
RankedSellers AS (
    SELECT Revenue, NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY Revenue DESC) as Decile
    FROM SellerSales
)
SELECT
    Decile,
    FORMAT(SUM(Revenue), 'C', 'en-US') as Total_Revenue,
    FORMAT(SUM(Revenue) * 1.0 / (SELECT SUM(Revenue) FROM SellerSales), 'P') as Share_of_Total
FROM RankedSellers
GROUP BY Decile
ORDER BY Decile;

05 · Executive roadmap & capital allocation

Stop the leaks first,
then compound.

A strategy is a choice about order, not a list of good ideas. Horizon 1 fixes leakage that costs revenue today without new capital. Horizon 2 builds compounding assets (retention, seller incubation, cross-sell) that only pay off once the leaks are sealed. Running them the other way round would pour cash into a broken funnel.

Stop leakage and protect core GMV. Four moves that need no new capital, each with a KPI gate.

Plan

How I take a marketplace from raw data to a decision

Diagnose the commercial problem, not the symptom

Look past the headline (7.5× GMV growth) to the constraint underneath: a 2018 plateau, 96.9% churn, 67.6% supply concentration. The headline is what the board already knows; the constraint is what it needs to see.

Isolate the pain point and its true owner

Decompose end-to-end workflows to find where the metric actually breaks (carrier transit, not seller pack time), so the recommendation names a specific team and lever, not an industry.

Design for unit economics, not just GMV

Gate retention spend to happy cohorts, solve freight traps with basket thresholds, and size demand before committing CAPEX: every recommendation is tested against the P&L, not only volume.

Build the operations that make the plan runnable

Ship each recommendation with a committed decision gate, a KPI the board can watch and a holdout comparison, so leadership can act, iterate or stop on evidence, not on faith.
